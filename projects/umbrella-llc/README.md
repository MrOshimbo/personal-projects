# Umbrella LLC

## Overview

Explore creating an umbrella limited liability company (LLC) that could provide a shared business structure for multiple ventures over time.

One possible venture discussed so far is organizing and producing paid events for bands. The broader idea is to have a flexible business home for more than one business or revenue-generating project, rather than treating each idea as completely unrelated.

This is an early-stage concept, not a decision to form an LLC.

## Initial concept

- **Umbrella structure:** Investigate whether one parent or umbrella LLC could support multiple ventures.
- **Live music events:** Explore producing paid events for bands, including the business model and practical operating requirements.
- **Future ventures:** Leave room for additional business ideas as they develop.

## First live-event concept

**Working concept:** A small, intimate, youth-oriented live-music showcase featuring multiple bands performing sequentially, with the possibility of a DJ opening the event.

**Intended audience:** Primarily high-school-age attendees, with some college-age attendees.

**Working minimum age:** 14+. This is the current planning direction, not a final admission policy; confirm venue requirements and operating details before publishing or selling tickets.

**Timing to explore:** A Saturday early-evening event, provisionally around 5:00–9:00 PM. This is a planning starting point, not a confirmed event schedule.

**Planning considerations for younger attendees:**
- Confirm in writing that the venue permits attendees age 14 and older and the intended event format.
- Consider an alcohol-free event for the initial show.
- Plan appropriate adult staffing, check-in, security, emergency procedures, parent-facing information, and safe pickup/transportation.
- Build the running order so the event finishes at a reasonable hour for high-school attendees.

### Initial age-verification approach

The goal is to verify that each attendee is at least 14 without creating unnecessary barriers at the door.

- Use an age check at entry, supported by a clearly published list of acceptable proof.
- Do not rely on a high-school ID as the only option: some school IDs do not show a date of birth, and not every attendee will have one available.
- Consider accepting a school ID that clearly shows date of birth, a driver's license or state ID, or a passport. A parent/guardian confirmation process could be considered for younger attendees who lack photo ID.
- Have the venue approve the final policy and train door staff to apply it consistently.
- Avoid retaining copies or photographs of identity documents unless a qualified legal adviser and the venue establish a specific need and compliant process.

This is a preliminary operational recommendation, not a statement of a universal legal ID standard. The final policy should be agreed with the venue and reviewed for applicable local requirements.

### First milestone: Set the age policy

**Status:** Working direction selected — plan around 14+ for the first event, subject to venue confirmation and a finalized age-verification process.

The age policy should be confirmed before venue selection, ticketing, and detailed budgeting.

## Questions to explore

1. What kinds of ventures might belong under the umbrella?
2. Would one LLC with separate DBAs be appropriate, or would separate subsidiary LLCs provide better separation?
3. What liability, insurance, licensing, tax, accounting, and contractual considerations would apply to live music events?
4. What would the event business look like financially—venues, ticketing, artist agreements, promotion, production costs, and revenue sharing?
5. At what point would the idea justify formation costs and ongoing administration?

These are research questions, not legal or tax conclusions. Get advice from qualified legal and tax professionals before choosing or forming a structure.
